## **1.8. Matter as the Source of Spacetime**

The presence of matter generates both space and time. A finite continuum emerges as long as relative mass densities do not deviate too strongly from each other.

* **Matter/Energy ↔ Spacetime**  
   In General Relativity, matter does not merely produce a field – it **defines the very structure of spacetime**. Without matter/energy, there would be no curved continuum.  
* **Finite Continuum**  
   As long as mass densities remain moderately distributed, a consistent, connected spacetime (continuum) forms, where causality and proper times can still be meaningfully defined.  
* **Boundary Case (extreme deviations of density)**  
   When mass densities diverge extremely (e.g. black hole collapse), this continuum locally disintegrates:  
   – outside, time remains measurable,  
   – inside, causality breaks apart, as space and time decouple at a singular point.

This is essentially an ontological reading of General Relativity: **space and time are not fundamental, but consequences of the distribution of matter**.

Matter “stretches” the stage of space and time, but only as long as the differences are not too extreme – otherwise the stage collapses.

This leads to the further question: where does matter come from? This question lies beyond classical GR, which assumes matter as given and only describes geometry.

It can be unfolded into different layers:

* **Classical GR**  
  * Postulate: matter/energy exists.  
  * Task: it generates curvature, which in turn determines motion.  
  * No statement on *where* matter originates.  
* **Cosmology (Standard Model, Big Bang)**  
  * Matter arises in the early phase from quantum fluctuations and symmetry breaking (e.g. Higgs mechanism, baryogenesis).  
  * Yet this presupposes spacetime already exists, in which these fluctuations occur.  
* **Quantum Gravity (e.g. LQG, String Theory)**  
  * Hypothesis: matter and spacetime emerge together from a deeper pre-geometric state.  
  * In many models, matter is not fundamental, but a **collective excitation** (like quasiparticles in a solid).  
  * Space, time, and matter would then be different aspects of the same underlying structure.  
* **Philosophical interpretation (as in ISE)**  
  * Matter is nothing but a manifestation of differentiation (information / energy condensation).  
  * Space and time are emergent descriptions of this differentiation.  
  * The question of origin becomes void, since matter is never created, but appears as a consequence of differentiation.

From the perspective of established physics, the question *“Where does matter come from?”* remains unanswered.  
 The honest answer is: **we do not know**.

Yet most modern approaches suggest that matter did not arise *from nothing*, but is instead a collective manifestation of a deeper structure.

**Matter as Differentiation from Nothing**

In the ISE, matter originates from a differentiation of Nothing – and this is striking, because the ISE perspective is not as exotic as it sounds. Many serious models in modern physics resemble this idea in surprising ways:

* **Quantum Field Theory / Vacuum Fluctuations**  
   The "nothing" (vacuum) is never empty, but filled with zero-point fluctuations. Matter can emerge as a condensed or stabilized fluctuation → "something" from "nothing."  
* **Loop Quantum Cosmology**  
   Before the universe, no classical spacetime exists, only a quantized nothing. Matter and space arise together at the "bounce."  
* **String Theory / Brane Cosmology**  
   The "nothing" is a pre-given state of deeper degrees of freedom (e.g. strings, branes). Matter arises as vibration states of these structures – likewise a differentiation from the nothing of ordinary spacetime.

ISE frames this more radically:

* "Nothing" \= Karii.  
* Matter does not arise from something pre-existing, but from the first differentiation – the breaking of absolute symmetry into distinctions.  
* Space and time follow only *afterwards* as scale sequences.

Thus, the statement "matter from nothing" is not only poetic, but structurally consistent with leading hypotheses – except that ISE formulates it ontologically stricter: differentiation as principle, not exception.

When reflected upon further, matter is first only electromagnetic repulsion. More deeply, matter is rest mass – an interaction potential with a certain energy. With fixed ratios, this rest mass can appear differently at different scales.

* **Everyday view**  
   Matter \= something with volume, repulsion, stability → practically **electromagnetic repulsion** of electron shells.  
* **Deeper physical view**  
   Matter \= something with **rest mass**.  
  * Mass \= coupling to gravitation.  
  * Rest mass \= energy present even without motion (E \= mc²).  
  * Rest mass is not a substrate, but an **interaction potential**: it defines how strongly a field (Higgs, EM, gravity) reacts to a particle.  
* **Scaling**  
   With fixed constants, rest mass can manifest differently depending on scale:  
  * atomic as electromagnetic repulsion,  
  * nuclear as strong interaction,  
  * cosmic as gravitational effect.

In ISE terms:  
 Matter \= "condensed differentiation," a **scale-bound manifestation** of energy.  
 Rest mass is the measure of how strongly this differentiation is retained within a given scale frame.

This matches the concise formulation: **matter is not a thing, but a relation.**

**Differentiation as Principle**

When one says *“Matter \= rest mass \= interaction potential”*, this is already **one level deeper** than an axiom.

An axiom in ISE would rather state:

* **“Differentiation generates states.”**

And only *derived from this*:

* certain states → define energy,  
* stabilized energy in relation → defines rest mass,  
* rest mass \+ relation → manifest for us as “matter.”

Thus, matter is **not an origin**, but a **derived relation**, as just described. Therefore in ISE it must not be treated as an axiom, but only as a **consequence of differentiation**.

Rest mass itself can be differentiated. A portion of rest mass can be split into two parts, establishing a distance between them. This distance corresponds to an energy and a relation between the two portions of rest mass. The system can then be recombined, converting the potential energy back into rest mass.

This mechanism describes exactly how **space** and **matter** are coupled through differentiation. It can be expressed as a chain:

* **Rest mass as potential**  
   – Rest mass is stored energy, stable in one state.  
* **Differentiation of rest mass**  
   – Rest mass can be split into two subsystems.  
   – Between them, a **distance** (spatial separation) is established.  
* **Distance as energy**  
   – This distance is not empty but carries an energy relation (binding or potential energy).  
   – Thus, space itself becomes a function of energy.  
* **Recombination**  
   – Bringing the parts back together folds the distance-energy (potential) again into rest mass.

**Consequence:**

* Space is nothing but **“externalized rest mass in the form of energy relations.”**  
* Matter and space are therefore not separate: one is stabilized differentiation, the other the “placed in between” differentiation.

This is exactly what ISE emphasizes: differentiation can appear **internally** (mass) or **externally** (distance/space), yet both are the same operation on different scales.

**Formalism of Differentiation**

In ISE-notation this can be expressed as:

$$M_0 \;\;\longleftrightarrow\;\; (M_1 + M_2) + D(E)$$

* $M_0$ = original rest mass (undifferentiated)  
* $M_1, M_2$ \= differentiated portions of the rest mass  
* $D(E)$ = distance $D$, which itself exists as an energy relation $E$

**Interpretation:**  
 An undivided rest mass is equivalent to two divided parts plus a distance, stored as energy. The recombination is the reverse transformation:

$$(M_1 + M_2) + D(E) \;\;\longrightarrow\;\; M_0$$

This is not an energy conservation law in the classical sense, but a **conservation of differentiation**: differentiation (whether internal as rest mass or external as distance/energy) is never lost, it merely changes form.

In the most compact ISE form:

$$\Delta = M + D$$

* $\Delta$ = total differentiation (indestructible, invariant)  
* $M$ = rest mass (internal differentiation)  
* $D$ = distance/energy (external differentiation)

**Principle:**  
 *Differentiation is conserved. It appears either as rest mass or as distance/energy.*

This serves as the ISE analogue to $E=mc^2$, but more general:

* $E=mc^2$ connects only mass and energy.  
* $\Delta = M + D$ shows both are different realizations of the same differentiation.

Thus matter = “bound differentiation,” space = “externalized differentiation.”

We start from the ISE core formula:  
$$\Delta = M + D$$

**Energy Conservation as Special Case**

* Set $M$ = rest mass (internal differentiation).   
* Set $D$ = distance with energy content (external differentiation).  
   If mass decreases ($M \downarrow$), distance-energy must increase ($D \uparrow$) so that $\\Delta$ remains constant.  
   This is **energy conservation**:
$$\Delta = \text{const} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Delta M = - \Delta D$$

This matches physical transformations:

* rest mass → radiation energy  
* binding energy → mass increase, etc.

**Gravitational Law from Differentiation**

* Differentiation $D$ means *divided rest mass* at distance $r$.  
* The energy of the distance is proportional to the ratio of mass portions and inversely proportional to distance.

ISE formulation:
$$D(E) \sim \frac{M_1 \, M_2}{r}$$

Taking the negative derivative with respect to $r$ gives:
$$F \sim -\frac{M_1 M_2}{r^2}$$

This is **Newtonian gravitation** – not as an axiom, but as a *necessary form of conservation of differentiation* when rest mass is transformed into distance.

**Result:**

* **Energy conservation** follows directly from constancy of $\Delta$.  
* **Gravitation** follows from the necessity that differentiation stored as separation must appear with $1/r$ potential character.

It must be noted that the division into “internal” (rest mass) and “external” (distance/energy) is **only perspectival**, not fundamental.

* From the perspective of one observer, something appears as “bound mass.”  
* From another scale or relation, the same differentiation appears as “distance/energy.”

Therefore, in ISE strictly speaking it is not:
$$\Delta = M + D \quad (\text{internal + external})$$

but simply:

$$\Delta = \Delta$$

And depending on context, this one differentiation decomposes into components that can appear as rest mass or distance.

Thus “mass” and “space” are not categories, but **interpretations** of the same differentiation.

In this context, the statement that “differentiation is indestructible, invariant” must be read as a formal property of the terms within the model, not as a categorical assertion about differentiation in general. The invariance of $\Delta = M + D$ refers only to the redistribution between internal (mass) and external (distance/energy) differentiation under the given assumptions. It does not imply that differentiation itself can never be undermined, reversed, or dissolved into indistinguishability. In spatial separation scenarios, differentiation may be redistributed or transformed in ways that are no longer reversible within the model’s framework. Thus, the invariance is a structural rule of the formalism, not a metaphysical claim about absolute indestructibility.
