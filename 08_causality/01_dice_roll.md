## **8.1. Example: Dice Roll**

This example illustrates one way to read causality along the lines developed here: the outcome is not pre-determined as an objective fact independent of observation, but is established through the interaction between energy states and the observer's collapse of possibilities into a single observed reality.

The **Dice Roll** is used as a thought experiment to illustrate how **causality and randomness** can be described relative to the observer's perspective. Detailed breakdown:

* **The Dice Roll Example and Causality**:  
  * The outcome of a dice roll is determined at the exact moment it stops. This event is analogous to the collapse of a quantum wave function — once the dice settles, its state is no longer probabilistic but definite.  
  * The observer's act of observing or interacting with a system "selects" one specific outcome among all possible states. This does **not mean that all possibilities simultaneously exist** or that alternative realities occur. The observer’s perspective collapses the event into one specific reality. Other potential outcomes do not form parallel realities — they neither exist nor don’t exist simultaneously.  
* **No Continuum Between Realities**:  
  * The dice roll metaphor suggests that different interpretations or realities do not exist as a continuum. Multiple states do not co-exist in parallel universes; they are exclusive to the observer’s perspective.  
* **Causality and the Observer**:  
  * The observer plays a central role in collapsing potential outcomes into definite states. The moment the dice is stopped corresponds to **the selection of a specific outcome** by the observer, without alternative possibilities remaining accessible through time or space.  
  * Causality, read in this way, emerges from this interaction. The action (the dice roll) is linked to an outcome through the observer's presence and selection, rather than through parallel outcomes in other universes. In later parts of the thesis this is referred to as constructive scale interference or scale coupling.

The emergence of **randomness and causality** stems from the continuous differentiation of energy across scales, which inherently drives the universe's evolution. This process brings randomness into play through fluctuations in potential energy, and causality emerges as a result of the relative interactions between these differentiated states.

**Randomness:**

* **Randomness** manifests through quantum fluctuations within the scale-free quantum field. These fluctuations are akin to what is observed in the Gaussian uncertainty principle, and as the scale increases, these quantum effects become normalized to macroscopic levels. Therefore, **quantum effects at smaller scales introduce randomness**, but at larger scales, they contribute to structured reality.

**Causality:**

* **Causality** can be read as arising from the continuous interaction of energy states within an expanding system. Time is treated here not as an intrinsic, linear dimension but as an emergent property developing from differentiated energy states. As the universe differentiates, **causal relationships** form through the **relative order** of those states, making causality a byproduct of the structure and differentiation of energy.  
* Causality then appears **driven by the relational dynamics** between energy states rather than by a classical chronological sequence. This **causal structure is interwoven with the emergent flow of time** from one differentiated state to another, rather than predicated on a universal arrow of time.

By understanding these concepts, we see that randomness reflects quantum-level fluctuations, while causality emerges through the evolving differentiation of potential energy in a scale-free, constantly expanding universe.

The picture outlined here overlaps in several ways with the **Block World theory** regarding space, time, and the nature of reality. Key parallels:

* **Time as a Relational Concept**:  
  * In both views, time is not treated as an absolute flowing dimension but as a relational construct. Here, time emerges from differentiated states of energy; Block World theory views time as a fixed dimension where all events — past, present, and future — exist simultaneously within a "block" of spacetime.  
* **Non-Unfolding Reality**:  
  * Both accounts depart from a conventional unfolding of the universe. In the present reading, reality emerges from an ongoing differentiation process; Block World theory holds that all events are already laid out across spacetime, waiting to be "observed" or experienced.  
* **Observer-Dependent Reality**:  
  * Here, the observer interacts with differentiated energy states, giving rise to the perception of time and reality. Similarly, Block World theory treats the passage of time as an illusion dependent on the observer's movement through the spacetime block.  
* **Relational Causality**:  
  * Causality in both readings is not tied to a flowing timeline. The present account links causality to the relationship between different energy states — analogous to how Block World theory treats causality as a spatial relationship within the block, without sequential time.  
* **Eternalism**:  
  * Both reject a present-driven reality. Under the present interpretation, reality is continuously differentiating and evolving across scales, consistent with a non-temporal, ever-existing universe. Block World theory adheres to eternalism, where all points in time are equally real and do not "come into being" in a linear fashion.

In short, Block World theory presents reality as a fixed spacetime structure, while the account given here emphasizes the dynamic differentiation of potential energy. Both reframe the conventional flow of time and share commonalities in how they treat time, causality, and the observer's role in shaping perceived reality.
