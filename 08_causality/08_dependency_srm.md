## **8.8. Scale-Relational Formalization of Dependency Propagation**

This chapter provides the formal bridge between the conceptual framework of dependency propagation (Chapter "Dependency Propagation") and Scale-Relational Mathematics (SRM). We establish that the continuous scale group admits discrete observables through self-resonance conditions, that the photon serves as the natural torsor origin without privileging any absolute unit, and that coupling conditions between scales remain ontologically open rather than derivable from mathematical structure alone.

**From Dependency Structure to S-Equivariant Formalism**

Chapter "Dependency Propagation" established that physical reality consists of dependency propagation through differentiation structure, with time, mass, space, and gravitation emerging as projective aspects of this singular process. The reductive sequence — from protoinformation through differentiation to observable phenomena — requires formal expression within a framework that respects scale symmetry as primitive.

SRM provides precisely this framework. Where classical mathematics smuggles in absolute units as hidden axioms, SRM treats ratios as primitive and scale transformations as fundamental. The task now is to express the dependency ontology in S-equivariant terms.

**The Scale Group and Photon-Relative Weights**

Let $S \cong (\mathbb{R}_{>0}, \times)$ denote the continuous multiplicative scale group. Following SRM-2 (Relational Primitives), a unit system constitutes an S-torsor $U$: a set with free and transitive S-action. No section of this torsor is privileged.

The photon, as the entity with minimal dependency depth, provides a natural but non-privileged origin for the torsor. A photon possesses minimal dependency structure — effectively a single node — and consequently exhibits no internal seriality, no mass, and propagation at the base rate $c$. This is not an absolute unit but a structural anchor: the simplest possible differentiation pattern.

**Definition (S-Weight):**
For a differentiation pattern $P$ with dependency depth $d(P)$, the S-weight is defined as:

$$w(P) = d(P) \cdot w_\gamma$$

where $w_\gamma$ denotes the photon weight. Since the photon represents the minimal differentiation ( $d(\gamma) = 1$), we have $w_\gamma = 1$ in photon-relative coordinates.

This definition satisfies SRM-3 (Equivariance): under scale transformation $\lambda \in S$, the weight transforms covariantly, and morphisms between patterns respect S-weights.

**Continuity and Discreteness: The Resonance Condition**

A central insight emerges from recognizing that protoinformation is continuous but becomes discrete through self-resonance. The differentiation substrate admits arbitrary scale values $\lambda \in S$, yet physical observables correspond only to those values that achieve resonant closure with themselves.

**Definition (Self-Resonance):**
A scale value $\lambda \in S$ is self-resonant if it forms a closed interaction domain — that is, if $\lambda$ can interact coherently only with itself and not with neighboring scale values.

**Axiom SRM-6 (Resonance Closure):**
Physically observable states correspond to self-resonant orbits in $S$. The stabilizer of an observable state under the resonance relation contains only that state itself:

$$\text{Stab}_\lambda^{\text{Res}} = {\lambda}$$

This axiom captures the phenomenon that a pattern at scale 1.5 (in photon-relative units) cannot couple with a pattern at scale 1.0 — not because of any arithmetic property of the ratio, but because each forms a closed resonance domain. The apparent quantization of physics (Planck quanta, energy levels, particle masses) emerges as self-resonance of the underlying continuous differentiation, not as fundamental discreteness.

**The Photon as Trivial Stabilizer**

The photon occupies a unique position: with dependency depth 1, it is trivially self-resonant. No internal structure exists that could interfere with itself. The photon thus represents the boundary case where resonance closure is automatic rather than emergent.

This explains why $c$ functions as the base rate of dependency propagation: one update per one base time unit is the irreducible operation. The photon does not travel at $c$; rather, $c$ is defined by the photon's structural simplicity.

**Scale Isolation Without Privileged Arithmetic**

A critical clarification must be stated: the conditions under which two scale values $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ can couple are not derivable from the structure of $S$ alone.

One might be tempted to assert that rational ratios permit coupling while irrational ratios enforce isolation. This would privilege $\mathbb{Q}$ as ontologically fundamental — a violation of SRM-4 (Relational Classification), which establishes that rationality, algebraicity, and transcendence are defined relative to a chosen base field, with no base privileged.

**Principle (Coupling Agnosticism):** The formal framework establishes:

* That self-resonance is the condition for observability
* That scale isolation exists as a phenomenon
* That S-weight provides a relational measure of dependency depth

The framework does not establish:

* Which scale ratios permit coupling
* Whether coupling conditions follow any arithmetic pattern
* Any privileged role for $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, or any other number-theoretic structure

To claim otherwise would import anthropological conventions into ontological structure.

**Time as Resonance Chain Serialization**

Chapter "Dependency Propagation" described time as the seriality of dependency itself. Within SRM, this receives precise expression through the scale-covariant derivative.

The derivative $D = d + \omega$, where $\omega$ is a 1-form valued in the Lie algebra of $S$ (for $S = \mathbb{R}_{>0}$, this is $\mathbb{R}$), splits into invariant and gauge parts. The curvature $F = d\omega$ encodes scale anomalies — points at which resonance closure fails.

Crucially, this derivative does not describe evolution *in* time as an external parameter. Rather, the resonance chain itself *is* the seriality. Time emerges from the ordering of resonance closure events, not as a container within which such events occur. The "update" of dependency propagation is not a temporal process but a structural relation that, when serialized from a particular scale perspective, projects as temporal sequence.

**Mass as S-Weight**

The identification of mass with dependency depth translates directly into SRM:

$$m(P) \propto w(P) = d(P) \cdot w_\gamma$$

A proton, with its complex internal dependency structure, possesses high S-weight. This weight is not a property the proton "has" but a characterization of how much differentiation the pattern contains. Inertia — resistance to acceleration — corresponds to the update overhead required for restructuring high-weight dependency networks.

Gravitational coupling between patterns becomes interaction between S-weights, with the propagation rate constrained by $c$ as the invariant base operation.

**The Resolution of the Update Circularity**

Chapter "Dependency Propagation"'s language of "updates" and "propagation" risked implying a temporal process external to the structure being described. SRM dissolves this circularity.

There is no update \*occurring in time\*. The dependency structure simply \*is\* its own transformation — or more precisely, what appears as transformation is a projection effect arising from the network of potential vectors standing in mutual dependency. Resonance closure is not an event but a condition: those $\lambda \in S$ satisfying self-resonance project as observables; those failing to close do not appear at all.

The apparent dynamism of physics — particles moving, fields evolving, systems transforming — emerges from the serialization of static resonance relations when viewed from within a particular scale. This serialization is time. No external clock is required; no computational substrate performs operations.

**Implications for Quantum Mechanics**

The wave function, reinterpreted as update state distribution in Chapter "Dependency Propagation", now receives S-equivariant expression. The probability distribution over a massive particle's position reflects the resonance state of its dependency network — which closure paths remain viable.

Superposition corresponds to multiple viable resonance paths. Measurement corresponds to coupling with an apparatus whose own resonance structure forces path selection. Entanglement corresponds to shared resonance structure across spatially separated nodes — correlations that require no signal propagation because the entangled system constitutes a single resonance domain.

Decoherence occurs when environmental coupling — itself a vast resonance network — overwhelms internal path options and selects definite outcomes.

**Conclusion: Mathematics Describes, Does Not Dictate**

SRM provides the formal language for expressing ISE's dependency ontology without importing absolute units or privileged arithmetic. The framework establishes structural relationships — S-weights, resonance conditions, covariant derivatives — while remaining agnostic about coupling dynamics.

This agnosticism is not a limitation but a feature. To derive coupling conditions from mathematical structure alone would be to confuse the map with the territory. The mathematics describes the form of scale-relational physics; the specific coupling relations constitute empirical content that no formal framework can supply a priori.

Just as Riemannian geometry provided the mathematical stage for general relativity without determining the value of $G$, SRM provides the mathematical stage for ISE without determining which scales interact. The constants of nature, including whatever structure governs scale coupling, remain empirical discoveries rather than mathematical necessities.
