## **10.1. Baryon asymmetry**

**Note on the Relevance of this Chapter**

This chapter, originally developed as part of the exploration into baryonic asymmetry within the Lambda-CDM framework, reflects an earlier phase of understanding. Recent advancements, including alternative perspectives provided in the accompanying analysis on the scalar differentiation of energy within the framework, suggest additional dimensions to consider. While this chapter remains an integral part of the thesis to provide historical and theoretical context, readers are encouraged to reference the posits in the further developed chapter.

Baryon asymmetry refers to the imbalance between matter and antimatter in the universe. According to the Big Bang, equal amounts of baryons (matter) and antibaryons (antimatter) should have been created, which would have led to mutual annihilation. However, the universe is overwhelmingly made of matter, which remains a mystery in physics.

Baryon asymmetry is not addressed directly at this stage; a few speculative hints might nevertheless connect to the topic:

* **Protoinformation and Differentiation**: If reality emerges from the differentiation of protoinformation and energy, baryon asymmetry might reflect a deeper difference in the rate or mechanism by which energy differentiates into matter versus antimatter — an inherent asymmetry in how energy splits into these two forms at a more fundamental level.  
* **Continuous Differentiation**: Read as a continuous flow and differentiation process rather than a singular origin, the conditions leading to matter-antimatter symmetry breaking may have arisen before the universe transitioned into its currently observed state, implying continuity with prior scales.  
* **Fractal and Multiversal Structures**: In a speculative multiverse with fractal structure, the baryon asymmetry in our region could be a local feature, while other regions might exhibit the opposite ratio — making the asymmetry a local characteristic within a larger differentiated cosmic context.

The speculative remarks retained below document the early-stage reasoning from which the mature treatment grew; they should not be read as current theoretical positions.**
