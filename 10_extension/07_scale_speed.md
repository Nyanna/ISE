## **10.7. Superluminal Speeds, Scale Shifts, and the Fate of Information**

The notion of **superluminal motion** — an object moving faster than the speed of light — is used here as a thought experiment to probe the behavior of **scale isolation** at the edge of a scale's kinematic range.

**Superluminal Travel and Scale Transitions: A Thought Experiment**

A speculative reading is that if an object could reach **superluminal speeds**, it might cross into a **higher scale** whose parameters for space, time, and velocity differ from those of the current one. Relative to that scale its motion could register near **zero velocity**, with motion and time redefined by the new space-time dynamics. Read as analogy, superluminal travel would then not violate physical law but reflect a shift in the **relative experience of motion and time** tied to scale.

This reading remains speculation. Scale isolation, as used throughout the preceding chapters, treats scales as operationally independent: there is no established channel that lets an object cross from one scale's space-time into another and continue as the same object. Superluminal transition is therefore kept as a thought experiment rather than a mechanism within the argument presented here — an object that leaves its scale, under this assumption, does not re-enter it.

**The Destruction of Information in Scale Shifts**

A related question is what happens to **information** — in the sense of the physical structure of an object — when such a transition is contemplated. Information defined against one scale's space-time relations has no obvious carrier outside of those relations, so a **complex information system** crossing scales would, on this reading, not survive intact.

Approaching superluminal speeds can then be pictured as accelerating toward the kinematic boundary of the current scale, where its space-time relations no longer support the object's configuration. Instead of emerging in a new scale, the object and its information would **dissolve** at that boundary, analogous in outcome (though not in mechanism) to loss behind a black-hole horizon. Under strict scale isolation, the disconnection is one-way: once the boundary is crossed, the information associated with the original scale is not recoverable from within it.

**Conclusion**

Taken together, superluminal transition between scales is an interesting analogy but not a mechanism the argument here endorses: scale isolation makes it inaccessible in principle. The likely outcome of any such attempted transition, on the reading developed above, is **informational dissolution** rather than transfer. This marks the boundary of the scale-interaction picture used in this thesis — what occurs beyond that boundary is left open.
