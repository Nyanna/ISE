
### **3.6.4. The Infinite Speed of Light**

**Beyond the Classical View of c**

The speed of light in vacuum, denoted as $c$, is traditionally understood as a finite universal constant approximately equal to $299,792,458$ m/s. However, this classical interpretation overlooks fundamental philosophical and mathematical inconsistencies that emerge when examining the self-referential nature of $c$ and its role in defining our measurement standards. This chapter explores the proposition that $c$ represents a **categorical positive infinity** rather than a finite constant, and examines the implications of this reconceptualization for deriving fundamental scales in physics.

**The Self-Referential Paradox of c**

**Circular Definitions in Modern Metrology**

The contemporary definition of the meter reveals a profound circularity in our understanding of $c$:

- The meter is defined as the distance traveled by light in vacuum during $\frac{1}{299,792,458}$ seconds
- The speed of light $c$ is then expressed as $299,792,458$ meters per second
- This creates a self-referential loop where $c$ defines the meter, which in turn defines $c$

This circularity suggests that $c$ is not merely a measurable quantity but rather the **fundamental relational constant** that establishes the connection between spatial and temporal dimensions.

**The Arbitrary Nature of Numerical Representation**

The numerical value $299,792,458$ for $c$ emerges not from any fundamental property of nature, but from historical conventions in defining the meter and second. In natural units, physicists often set $c = 1$, revealing that $c$ functions as the **conversion factor** between space and time rather than a traditional velocity.

**Infinite Divisibility and the Nature of Continuity**

**The Continuum Problem**

If space and time are truly continuous, as assumed in general relativity, then:

- Any finite spatial interval contains **uncountably infinite** points
- Any finite temporal interval contains **uncountably infinite** instants
- Light traverses infinitely many spatial points in any finite time interval

This leads to the fundamental question: How many "elementary units" of space does light traverse per "elementary unit" of time? The answer, under continuous assumptions, is **infinity divided by infinity** - a mathematically indeterminate expression.

**The Functional Infinity Argument**

Without a minimum velocity scale, the ratio $\frac{c}{v}$ can become arbitrarily large as $v \to 0$. This creates a situation where $c$ is **infinitely removed** from all achievable velocities, making it functionally equivalent to a positive infinity in the velocity space.

Read as an analogy, $c$ then plays the role of the "point at infinity" that completes the projective geometry of velocity space, comparable to how the complex plane is completed by adding a point at infinity.

**The ISE Perspective**

If the zero-point energy is indeed infinite, then our observable scales represent different "levels" or "multiples" of this underlying infinity. In this context:

- One meter contains "infinitely much space" as a measurable portion of infinite spatial energy
- One second contains "infinitely much time" as a measurable portion of infinite temporal energy
- $c$ becomes the **fundamental proportion** relating these infinite quantities

**Implications for Fundamental Scale Derivation**

**The Failure of Traditional Approaches**

Classical attempts to derive fundamental scales rely on combinations of dimensional constants:

- **Planck scale**: $l_P = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}}$
- **Compton wavelength**: $\lambda_C = \frac{\hbar}{mc}$
- **Classical electron radius**: $r_e = \frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 mc^2}$

However, if $c$ represents a categorical infinity, these expressions become problematic as they involve ratios of infinite quantities.

**Dimensionless Scales from Infinite Relations**

The reading suggested here is that fundamental scales emerge from **dimensionless relationships** between infinite quantities. Rather than seeking absolute minimum lengths or times, the focus shifts to **proportional structures** within the infinite relational context.

Elementary scales then appear not as absolute quantities but as **relative quantizations** or **reference levels** within the underlying infinite structure of spacetime.
