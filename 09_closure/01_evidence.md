## **9.1. On the Interpretive Character of Cosmological Evidence**

Large-scale cosmological claims are rarely settled by a single observation. The same phenomena that anchor the standard Big Bang narrative admit more than one consistent reading, and the present thesis is one such reading. What follows is not a rebuttal of established cosmology but a demonstration that several canonical "pieces of evidence" are compatible with a continuous, scale-differentiating picture as well as with a singular origin — which places much of the interpretive weight on narrative choice rather than on the data alone.

**Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)**

The **CMB** is traditionally presented as a "relic" radiation from the early universe, a cornerstone of Big Bang cosmology. It is argued that the CMB provides clear evidence of the hot, dense state of the early universe during **recombination** when photons decoupled from matter.

* Read more cautiously, the CMB is direct evidence of **recombination** — a cooling phase at which photons decoupled from matter. Recombination on its own does not fix the cause of the preceding hot, dense state. Within a scale-differentiating picture the CMB can be interpreted as an outcome of **energy differentiation across scales** rather than as a relic of a singular initial event; this is an interpretive, not a derivational, claim.
* The step from "CMB observed" to "Big Bang confirmed" therefore relies on an auxiliary assumption about origin. The observation itself constrains the thermal history of recombination; the inference to a single cosmic origin is an additional narrative layer.

**Redshift as Expansion of Voids**

Cosmological **redshift** is commonly read as evidence for the expansion of space as a whole. A void-centred reading is also available and fits the scale-differentiation picture used here.

* Under that reading, redshift can be attributed in part to the **expansion of underdense regions (voids)**, which matches an energy-differentiation view across cosmic scales. As voids grow and matter organises into filaments and clusters, observed redshift would reflect changing **energy states** along the line of sight rather than a uniform stretching of an underlying space.
* This reading is an interpretive variant, not a derivation. It does not require a singular inflationary origin; it also does not refute one. It places redshift on the evidence list for **expansion in a broad sense** while leaving the specific mechanism underdetermined by the data alone.

**Nucleosynthesis as a Condition for Uniform Element Distribution**

**Primordial nucleosynthesis**, often cited as evidence for the Big Bang, is the process that supposedly occurred in the first minutes of the universe, producing light elements like hydrogen, helium, and lithium.

* The observed abundances are consistent with Big Bang predictions and nucleosynthesis itself is not in dispute here. The reinterpretation offered is limited to framing: the process can be read as occurring **under specific conditions of energy differentiation**, without the narrative requirement of a singular triggering event. The **conditions for uniform light-element distribution** can arise in a scale-differentiating cosmos as well as in a standard hot-Big-Bang cosmos.
* Primordial nucleosynthesis therefore evidences a particular set of physical conditions; the link from those conditions to a singular cosmic origin is an additional step supplied by the surrounding model, not by the abundance data alone.

**Large-Scale Structures from Scaled Anisotropies**

Lastly, the **large-scale structure of the universe** — the distribution of galaxies, clusters, and voids — has been presented as evidence of inflation, which supposedly amplified **quantum fluctuations** into the anisotropies that later grew into galaxies and clusters.

* A scale-differentiation reading accommodates these structures as the outcome of **scaled anisotropies** — density contrasts that arise when energy redistributes across scales — without invoking inflation as a separate mechanism. What standard cosmology interprets as amplified quantum fluctuations can, in this reading, be framed as energy redistributing across cosmic scales and leaving anisotropies behind. The two readings differ in mechanism, not in the structures they account for.
* This is a familiar underdetermination point: the same large-scale observations are compatible with more than one theoretical account, and the data alone do not single out one as "proven".

**On Interpretation and Narrative**

Taken together, the canonical evidence for the Big Bang — CMB, redshift, nucleosynthesis, large-scale structure — admits a scale-differentiation reading as well. The observations themselves report **phenomena** (recombination, element formation, structure growth); the step from phenomena to a specific cosmological story is supplied by the surrounding framework.

The Big Bang picture is internally consistent and empirically anchored; it is also, as a whole, a **narrative** that joins these observations under the assumption of a singular origin. The account offered here is an alternative narrative that joins the same observations under continuous **energy differentiation across scales**. Both narratives are interpretive rather than strictly derived from the data, and the choice between them depends as much on which explanatory pattern one finds more useful as on the observations alone.

**An Instrumentalist Reading**

A reasonable question follows: what role is this thesis meant to play? Much of it does not issue sharp, numerical predictions that distinguish it from standard cosmology on a single observation; its contribution sits at the level of framing and organisation of known results.

An **instrumentalist** reading fits that role. On such a reading, a theoretical picture is judged less by whether it is "proven" by a decisive test and more by whether it organises data, guides inquiry, and generates useful questions.

* Instrumentalism treats theories as **useful tools** for organising and interpreting data rather than as claims about an ultimate reality. Under this view, judging a framework by predictive uniqueness is one criterion among several.
* On that criterion this thesis is best read as providing an alternative organisation of familiar observations — recombination, redshift, nucleosynthesis, structure formation — under a common scale-differentiation theme, and as a source of questions rather than a source of settled answers.

Attempting to confirm or rule out the thesis through a single cosmological test is therefore only partially informative: much of its content is interpretive rather than strictly predictive. Its usefulness lies in the framing it offers — in showing that familiar data admit more than one consistent story, and that the choice between stories is itself part of what cosmology does.
