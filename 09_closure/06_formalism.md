## **9.6. The Challenge of Mathematical Formalism**

Several assumptions embedded in the equations of current physics take a different role here. Time, space, energy, and force are interpreted as emergent properties of scale differentiation rather than fixed or absolute entities. This shift in interpretation makes a direct translation into existing mathematical formalisms non-trivial.

For instance, in general relativity, space and time are woven together in the fabric of spacetime, with gravity emerging as the curvature of this fabric. The scale-based reading treats space and time as emergent from deeper energy differentiations — manifestations of underlying processes rather than intrinsic primitives. Similarly, quantum mechanics relies on fixed constants and probabilistic functions whose domain of validity may need to be re-examined in a scale-dependent setting.

Adapting existing equations to this reading would require re-interpretations and adjustments — redefining foundational terms and reworking parts of the mathematical structure to reflect scale-dependent, emergent quantities. (Interpretation.)

A complementary route is to develop a mathematical language tailored to scale differentiation — an algebra in which scale-free fields, non-linear time flows, and emergent spatial dimensions can be expressed directly. Such a formalism would sit alongside, not replace, existing tools: it would aim to represent energy differentiation and the continuous propagation of potential energy in their natural variables. (Speculation.)

Concretely, this would involve formulating equations that handle scale-free fields, multi-dimensional time, and continuous differentiation of potential energy. The goal is not to supersede existing mathematical models but to provide a more direct expression of the principles discussed here, with the hope of yielding additional testable hypotheses.
