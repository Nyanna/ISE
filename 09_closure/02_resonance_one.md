## **9.2. Empirical Resonance — The Gjergo & Kroupa Study

**A Corroboration of Principles**

The theoretical framework of the ISE posits that the universe is not the product of a singular, temporal origin but is instead a manifestation of continuous, scale-relative differentiation. This chapter analyzes the findings of the 2025 study by Gjergo & Kroupa, which provides significant, independent empirical data that aligns with and substantiates the core tenets of ISE. While the framework does not require external validation, this study serves as a powerful case study in how observational data, when liberated from the narrative constraints of the standard cosmological model (CDM), naturally resonates with the principles of scale differentiation.

**The Study: A Detailed Overview**

The paper at the center of this analysis presents a fundamental challenge to the established interpretation of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB).

* **Title and Authorship:**  
  * **Title:** "The Impact of Early Massive Galaxy Formation on the Cosmic Microwave Background"  
  * **Authors:** Eda Gjergo (Nanjing University) and Pavel Kroupa (University of Bonn & Charles University).  
  * **Publication:** First published on arXiv on May 7, 2025 (arXiv:2505.04687), and subsequently in the journal *Nuclear Physics B*.  
* **Observational and Theoretical Foundations:**  
  * **JWST Findings:** The study is built upon observations from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), which detected massive elliptical galaxies at a redshift of z \> 13\. This places their formation less than 330 million years after the traditionally-posited Big Bang, a much earlier and more rapid development than predicted. The rapid, intense growth of these galaxies would have generated enormous amounts of radiation and dust.  
  * **Theoretical Modeling (IGIMF Theory):** The authors employed the Integrated Galaxy-wide Initial Mass Function (IGIMF) theory, developed by Kroupa et al. This model describes a top-heavy star formation process in early-type galaxies (ETGs), which allows for the high-intensity luminosity and rapid generation of α-process metals observed in these early structures.  
  * **Calculations on CMB Coverage:** The core of their argument is a calculation of the effect of this early galactic population. Dust within these galaxies, heated by the immense stellar radiation, would have emitted a strong thermal spectrum. This light, cosmologically redshifted by the universe's expansion, would contribute to the CMB energy density measured today. The authors calculate that even their most conservative estimates show this source accounting for at least 1.4% of the CMB's energy density, with the potential to account for the entire CMB under maximal assumptions.  
* **Data Basis at a Glance:**

| Data Source | Type of Data |
| ----- | ----- |
| JWST (NIRCam/NIRSpec) | Observations of massive ETGs at z \> 13 |
| Theoretical Models | IGIMF with a top-heavy IMF, chemical enrichment |
| Cosmological Calculation | Radiation transport and energy budgets |

* The authors computationally derived a galaxy radius of approximately 400 kpc, with formation occurring at z \~ 15-20. The central hypothesis is that thermalized light scattered by dust from these galaxies reaches the CMB level we observe today.  
* **Significance and Consequences:**  
  * The study argues that the CMB may not (solely) originate from the hot primordial plasma but could contain a substantial contribution from early galaxy radiation.  
  * The primary consequence is that the parameters of the ΛCDM model, which are derived from CMB analysis, would be potentially distorted or fundamentally incorrect.

**Deconstruction of the ΛCDM Model's Anchor**

The true significance of the Gjergo & Kroupa study lies in the cascading effect it has on the entire edifice of the ΛCDM model. Standard cosmology is not merely supported by the CMB; it is anchored to it.

The analysis of the CMB's anisotropies is the primary method used to determine the key parameters of the universe within the ΛCDM framework, including the precise proportions of dark energy and dark matter. This entire analysis, however, rests on the non-negotiable assumption that the CMB is of primordial origin.

By presenting a plausible, data-driven alternative for the CMB's origin, the study effectively saws at the anchor chain of the standard model. If the CMB is not a relic of the Big Bang plasma, but is instead a foreground generated by early structures, then the parameters derived from it are built on a false premise. This would mean that the calculated ratios of dark energy and dark matter are not fundamental properties of the universe, but artifacts of a profound misinterpretation. This resonates with the position that dark matter and dark energy are not new substances but emergent properties or misinterpretations of scale-dependent phenomena.

**Interpretation**

The findings of Gjergo & Kroupa do not "prove" the ISE, as ISE is a framework for interpretation rather than a model seeking conventional proof. However, the study provides powerful, real-world data that aligns perfectly with ISE's core postulates.

**The CMB as a Remnant of Scale Differentiation**

The framework has always maintained that the CMB is not the afterglow of a singular creation event. Instead, it is viewed as a remnant or imprint of past phases of energy differentiation on a cosmic scale. The Gjergo & Kroupa paper provides a concrete, physical mechanism for exactly such a process. The "early, massive galaxies" they describe are a perfect example of a significant "differentiation event" in the universe's history. Their thermalized light, distributed across the cosmos, represents precisely the kind of scale-relative energy equilibrium that ISE would predict to form a background radiation.

**The Dissolution of the Singular Big Bang Narrative**

ISE rejects the necessity of a singular beginning, viewing the Big Bang not as an absolute origin but as a localized differentiation event within an infinite process. The Gjergo & Kroupa study provides critical support for this position by weakening the primary evidence used to prop up the singularity narrative. If the CMB is not the "first light" from the moment after creation, then the most powerful piece of "evidence" for that specific narrative is removed. This aligns with the ISE's stance that cosmological evidence is often interpreted to fit a convenient story, rather than being an objective truth.

**Matter, Asymmetry, and Structure Formation**

The JWST's discovery of early, complex structures challenges the linear, time-gated predictions of the ΛCDM model. The framework, however, accommodates this easily. Since time itself is an emergent property of scale differentiation, there is no absolute timeline that dictates when complex structures can form. The early emergence of massive galaxies is entirely consistent with a model where differentiation can occur at varying rates across different scales and regions. These galaxies provide the baryonic matter necessary to generate the thermal radiation proposed in the study, a process that fits within the ISE's view of matter as condensed protoinformation.

**Dark Matter as a Scale-Shifted Artifact**

The framework posits that dark matter is an emergent property linked to differentiated energy states, not a new type of particle. It can be understood as a scale-shifted form of energy that still interacts gravitationally but is no longer electromagnetically active on our observational scale. The Gjergo & Kroupa study indirectly supports this by challenging the calculations that determine the *amount* of dark matter required by the ΛCDM model. If the CMB data is compromised, then the "need" for a specific ratio of dark matter is also compromised, opening the door for alternative explanations like that of the ISE, which avoids the contradictions inherent in the standard particle-based dark matter model.

**A Note on Predictive Corroboration**

It is noteworthy that the framework, as outlined in its 2024 formulation, made the qualitative prediction that the CMB should not be interpreted as a direct relic of a singular Big Bang event, but rather as the product of subsequent, large-scale differentiation processes within the universe.

The Gjergo & Kroupa study (2025), based on empirical JWST data, provides a specific and plausible physical mechanism for this exact postulation. By identifying the thermalized light from a vast population of early galaxies as a potential source for the CMB, the study offers a concrete validation of the ISE's prior conceptual claim. This represents a powerful instance where a theoretical framework, developed independently of the data, is later corroborated by the discovery of a corresponding physical process.

**From Theoretical Postulate to Empirical Resonance**

The Gjergo & Kroupa study represents a significant turning point in cosmology. It demonstrates that with new observational tools, long-held assumptions can be fundamentally challenged. For the Infinite Scale Expansion, this study provides a powerful empirical resonance. It offers a plausible physical mechanism — thermalized light from early galaxies — that corresponds directly to the ISE's abstract principle of the CMB as a relic of cosmic differentiation.

By undermining the primordial interpretation of the CMB, the study weakens the foundation of the ΛCDM model and its reliance on concepts like dark matter and dark energy, for which ISE provides a more coherent, integrated explanation. The study thus serves as a critical bridge, transforming the principles of ISE from a purely theoretical and ontological framework into one that is directly supported by, and can provide context for, the latest empirical discoveries in astrophysics.
