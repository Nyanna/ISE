## **9.2. Empirical Resonance — The Gjergo & Kroupa Study**

**A Point of Contact with Observation**

ISE treats the universe as a manifestation of continuous, scale-relative differentiation rather than the product of a singular temporal origin. This section discusses the 2025 study by Gjergo & Kroupa, whose empirical findings are consistent with that reading. The study is not taken as proof, but as an instance where observational data, interpreted outside the narrative of the standard cosmological model ($\Lambda$CDM), admits a reading compatible with scale differentiation.

**The Study: A Detailed Overview**

The paper at the center of this analysis presents a fundamental challenge to the established interpretation of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB).

* **Title and Authorship:**  
  * **Title:** "The Impact of Early Massive Galaxy Formation on the Cosmic Microwave Background"  
  * **Authors:** Eda Gjergo (Nanjing University) and Pavel Kroupa (University of Bonn & Charles University).  
  * **Publication:** First published on arXiv on May 7, 2025 (arXiv:2505.04687), and subsequently in the journal *Nuclear Physics B*.  
* **Observational and Theoretical Foundations:**  
  * **JWST Findings:** The study is built upon observations from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), which detected massive elliptical galaxies at a redshift of z \> 13\. This places their formation less than 330 million years after the traditionally-posited Big Bang, a much earlier and more rapid development than predicted. The rapid, intense growth of these galaxies would have generated enormous amounts of radiation and dust.  
  * **Theoretical Modeling (IGIMF Theory):** The authors employed the Integrated Galaxy-wide Initial Mass Function (IGIMF) theory, developed by Kroupa et al. This model describes a top-heavy star formation process in early-type galaxies (ETGs), which allows for the high-intensity luminosity and rapid generation of α-process metals observed in these early structures.  
  * **Calculations on CMB Coverage:** The core of their argument is a calculation of the effect of this early galactic population. Dust within these galaxies, heated by the immense stellar radiation, would have emitted a strong thermal spectrum. This light, cosmologically redshifted by the universe's expansion, would contribute to the CMB energy density measured today. The authors calculate that even their most conservative estimates show this source accounting for at least 1.4% of the CMB's energy density, with the potential to account for the entire CMB under maximal assumptions.  
* **Data Basis at a Glance:**

| Data Source | Type of Data |
| ----- | ----- |
| JWST (NIRCam/NIRSpec) | Observations of massive ETGs at z \> 13 |
| Theoretical Models | IGIMF with a top-heavy IMF, chemical enrichment |
| Cosmological Calculation | Radiation transport and energy budgets |

* The authors computationally derived a galaxy radius of approximately 400 kpc, with formation occurring at z \~ 15-20. The central hypothesis is that thermalized light scattered by dust from these galaxies reaches the CMB level we observe today.  
* **Significance and Consequences:**  
  * The study argues that the CMB may not (solely) originate from the hot primordial plasma but could contain a substantial contribution from early galaxy radiation.  
  * A consequence is that parameters of the $\Lambda$CDM model derived from CMB analysis would be shifted if part of the CMB signal is of later-structure origin.

**Deconstruction of the ΛCDM Model's Anchor**

The true significance of the Gjergo & Kroupa study lies in the cascading effect it has on the entire edifice of the ΛCDM model. Standard cosmology is not merely supported by the CMB; it is anchored to it.

The analysis of the CMB's anisotropies is the primary method used to determine the key parameters of the universe within the ΛCDM framework, including the precise proportions of dark energy and dark matter. This entire analysis, however, rests on the non-negotiable assumption that the CMB is of primordial origin.

By offering a data-driven alternative for part of the CMB's origin, the study loosens this anchoring. If some fraction of the CMB is a foreground generated by early structures rather than a pure primordial relic, the parameters derived under the purely primordial assumption require revision. In that case the inferred ratios of dark energy and dark matter may be partly artifacts of the interpretive assumption. This is compatible with the view — developed elsewhere in this thesis — that dark matter and dark energy can be read as emergent, scale-dependent phenomena rather than new substances.

**Interpretation**

The findings of Gjergo & Kroupa do not prove ISE — the thesis is an interpretive framework rather than a predictive model tested by a single observation. The study does provide an observational setting whose natural reading is consistent with several of ISE's postulates, which is what is examined in the following paragraphs.

**The CMB as a Remnant of Scale Differentiation**

Within ISE the CMB is read not as the afterglow of a singular creation event but as an imprint of past phases of energy differentiation on a cosmic scale. The Gjergo & Kroupa paper describes a concrete physical process of that character: the early, massive galaxies they model function as a large-scale differentiation event, and their thermalized, cosmologically redshifted light is the kind of distributed energy equilibrium that a scale-relative reading of the background would expect.

**The Dissolution of the Singular Big Bang Narrative**

ISE does not assume a singular beginning; it reads the Big Bang as a localized differentiation event within a broader, open-ended process. The Gjergo & Kroupa analysis is relevant to this reading because it softens the standard inference from the CMB to a unique primordial moment: if part of the CMB signal originates in early structures rather than in a first-light plasma, then the evidential chain from CMB to singular beginning is not as tight as it is often presented.

**Matter, Asymmetry, and Structure Formation**

JWST's detection of early, complex structures is in tension with the linear, time-gated structure-formation expectations of $\Lambda$CDM. On a reading in which time is an emergent property of scale differentiation, there is no absolute timeline fixing when complex structures must form: differentiation can proceed at varying rates across different scales and regions, and the early emergence of massive galaxies is compatible with that picture. These galaxies also supply the baryonic matter required to produce the thermal radiation discussed in the study, which connects back to the reading of matter as condensed protoinformation introduced earlier.

**Dark Matter as a Scale-Shifted Artifact**

Within ISE, dark matter is read as an emergent feature linked to differentiated energy states rather than a new particle species — a scale-shifted form of energy that still couples gravitationally but is no longer electromagnetically active at our observational scale. The Gjergo & Kroupa study is relevant here only indirectly: by affecting the inference chain that fixes the *amount* of dark matter required by $\Lambda$CDM. If part of the CMB signal is reassigned to early structures, the specific mass-energy ratios derived from the background become less tightly constrained, which leaves more room for scale-based readings of dark matter alongside the standard particle-based one.

**A Note on Prior Reading and Later Data**

The 2024 formulation of this thesis already carried the qualitative reading that the CMB should not be treated as a direct relic of a singular Big Bang event, but as an imprint of subsequent, large-scale differentiation processes within the universe.

The Gjergo & Kroupa study (2025), based on empirical JWST data, proposes a specific physical mechanism of that general type: thermalized light from a large population of early galaxies as a possible contributor to the CMB. This is not a validation — the study makes no claim about ISE — but it is a case in which an independently developed interpretive reading and a later, independently derived physical mechanism point in the same direction.

**From Prior Reading to Empirical Contact**

The Gjergo & Kroupa study is a notable moment in CMB cosmology: new observational data prompt a re-examination of assumptions that had previously been treated as settled. For the present thesis the study is useful as a concrete point of contact — a physical mechanism (thermalized light from early galaxies) that sits next to the more abstract reading of the CMB as an imprint of cosmic differentiation.

By weakening the assumption that the CMB is wholly primordial, the study also loosens some of the $\Lambda$CDM inferences that depend on that assumption, including the precise budgets of dark matter and dark energy. It does not replace those inferences, and it does not prove the scale-based readings offered here; it does widen the interpretive space in which those readings can be discussed alongside the standard model.
