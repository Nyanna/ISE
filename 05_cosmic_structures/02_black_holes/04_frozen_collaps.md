
### **5.2.4. Black Hole: The Frozen Interior Collapse**

Within the Schwarzschild radius resides the collapsing star at the very moment of its collapse. Extending outward toward the horizon, each layer represents an asymptotically further rotated state of the star’s surrounding matter. The farther from the center, the more advanced its proper time, and the faster it runs. At the core – at the would-be singularity – remains the initial fluctuation of critical density that triggered the collapse, not necessarily at the geometric center but wherever the first instability arose.

This description is not merely metaphorical but a physical reality: even before collapse, the star produces enormous gravitational influence and severe temporal distortion. The first fluctuation of critical density breaks the causal order of its scale. That point freezes in time relative to us. Surrounding matter then follows the collapse, quickly reaching critical density and freezing as well. This process continues outward, producing a cascade of frozen shells. From our external frame, the star as a whole becomes frozen at the moment of collapse, with time running faster the farther one moves outward from the center.

Thus the collapse never appears complete to us. Every layer of the star, upon reaching critical density, halts in our coordinate time. The sequence persists outward until the event horizon is formed. This corresponds to a strict *outside-observer foliation* of spacetime, where we do not track the proper time of the infalling matter but fix all events on constant-t hypersurfaces of Schwarzschild coordinates. In this view:

* The first fluctuation defines the innermost frozen region.  
* Outward layers follow, each with faster apparent time until the horizon is approached.  
* The interior remains fixed at the collapse moment in this coordinate picture.

Physically, however, invariants such as curvature and the infaller’s proper time indicate that the collapse completes in finite eigen-time. The frozen structure is therefore a coordinate manifestation. In alternative coordinate systems (Kruskal, Painlevé), no static interior exists. Yet for us outside, the star is optically and causally anchored to this state, validating the outside-frame interpretation.

This perspective can be cast into a clean coordinate model:

**Starting Point – External Coordinates**

We use the Schwarzschild metric in standard (t,r,θ,φ) outside the stellar surface:

$$(ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right) dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1} dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2)$$

Here t denotes the time of a stationary observer at infinity.

**Definition of the Freeze Front**

Let r_f(t) denote the radius of a shell that, at external time t, has just crossed the critical density threshold.

* Condition: local density ρ(r,t) → ρ_crit.  
* In Schwarzschild time, dr_f/dt → 0 as r_f → 2M.

**Cascade Structure**

When an inner shell r1 reaches critical density, it freezes in t-coordinates. A shell r2 \> r1 follows, also freezing after finite proper time, but appearing to us later.

* This creates a *static shell structure in t*: smaller r corresponds to earlier proper time but later external observation.  
* As r → 2M, t diverges logarithmically:  
  $$(t \sim -2M \ln\left|\frac{r}{2M} - 1\right| + const.)$$

**Interpretation**

* **Physical (Proper Time):** Collapse proceeds inward and completes in finite proper time.  
* **Optical/Causal (External Frame):** We perceive a frozen configuration, each outer shell moving faster relative to the inner ones, with time dilation decreasing outward.

**Confirmation from General Relativity**

Even within classical GR, the black hole is effectively frozen for us at the moment of collapse, forming what can be described as a *gradual sphere*. If we adopt the strict external observer’s perspective in Schwarzschild time $t$, the following holds:

* **From outside** we never witness the collapse as completed.  
* Each shell of matter freezes asymptotically at some radius $r > r_s$, the closer to the horizon, the stronger the time dilation.  
* This produces a layered, gradual sphere: inner regions frozen earlier, outer regions later, with the horizon as the final boundary.

From the standpoint of a distant observer, a black hole formed billions of years ago still appears locked in its collapse state, almost unchanged since formation. This is not a mere visual effect: time itself is genuinely frozen relative to our external frame. The collapse proceeds infinitely slowly in our coordinates due to the divergence of gravitational time dilation at the horizon.

In GR, the distinction arises between two views:

* **External frame (our time):** The proper time of infalling matter slows to zero as $r \to 2M$, making the process asymptotically eternal.  
* **Internal frame (falling matter):** Finite proper time elapses until $r=0$, where GR predicts the singularity.

Thus GR treats the frozen exterior as a coordinate effect, but in our interpretation this external time is the physically decisive frame. The gravitational timeframe serves as the objective reference, making the frozen collapse not only a perspectival illusion but a real physical condition.

**Reduction of Asymmetric Potentials and Breakdown of Causality**

Consider a collapsing object with an asymmetric mass distribution, for instance a cubic field configuration. In our interpretation, using the gravitationally measurable exterior as the objective reference frame, the asymmetry would persist in the external field until the collapse is complete in this objective timeframe. Since the collapse takes infinitely long in external time, the outer field would appear permanently imprinted with the initial asymmetry.

Classical GR, however, provides a different resolution. It predicts that all higher multipole moments beyond total mass, spin, and charge radiate away through gravitational waves. In such a picture:

* **Non-spherical fields generate gravitational waves.** Quadrupole and higher moments couple to radiative degrees of freedom, carrying away energy and information about asymmetry:  
  $$(P = \frac{G}{5c^5},\left\langle \dddot Q_{ij}\dddot Q^{ij} \right\rangle)$$

  where $Q_{ij}$ denotes the quadrupole moment of the mass distribution.  
* **Before horizon formation,** these multipoles decay via Regge–Wheeler and Zerilli dynamics, producing observable gravitational radiation and Bondi mass loss:  
  $$(\frac{dM_B}{du} = -\frac{1}{4\pi} \int |N(u,\Omega)|^2 d\Omega \leq 0)$$

  indicating a real reduction of multipole structure before stationarity is achieved.  
* **After horizon formation,** the black hole rings down in quasinormal modes, with damping times scaling with its mass. For Schwarzschild $l=2$ modes:  
  $$(\text{Im}(\omega)M \approx 0.089 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \tau \approx \frac{GM}{c^3},\frac{1}{\text{Im}(\omega)})$$

  Examples: $M=10 M_\odot \Rightarrow \tau \approx 0.55,\text{ms};; M=10^6 M_\odot \Rightarrow \tau \approx 55,\text{s}.$  
  Late-time tails follow Price’s law: $\psi_l(t) \sim t^{-(2l+3)}$.  
* **End state:** Only the lowest multipole moments survive, consistent with the no-hair theorem. A non-rotating black hole becomes perfectly spherical (Schwarzschild), a rotating one takes the Kerr form with $M_l + iS_l = M(ia)^l$.

Simplified, the process is: **tidal forces distort the asymmetry, motion generates gravitational waves, and radiation removes the non-spherical components.** The outcome is an exterior field that rapidly smooths into a symmetric form. Read through the external-frame lens used here, one might expect a permanent causal imprint of asymmetry frozen in external time; GR resolves this by radiating the asymmetries away before the horizon closes, so both descriptions converge on a symmetric exterior — by different routes.

**Link to Scale Differentiation**

In GR the cascade of collapse is space-symmetrizing: non-spherical contributions are radiated away before the horizon is fully formed, and by the time the event horizon exists, the exterior field is effectively symmetric. Read through the scale-differentiation lens used throughout ISE, differentiation is the carrier of spatial structure, and a black hole suppresses further differentiation for the external frame. As asymmetries vanish and the exterior settles into spherical form, what remains externally is the minimal differentiated structure still observable from outside. The reading differs from GR only in the interpretation of the interior: GR extrapolates toward a singularity, whereas the scale-differentiation reading treats the inner structure as persisting in its own frame, causally detached. The observable exterior effects are the same; the distinction is an interpretive choice about how to handle the causally inaccessible region, and harmonizes the GR-predicted reduction of asymmetry with the principle that space expresses scale differentiation.

**Internal Frames and Causal Separation**

General Relativity indeed treats the black hole interior as possessing its own causal structure, though it does not describe it as an “alternative frame.” Instead, it is formulated in terms of proper time and causal domains:

* **Causally separated interior:** Once the event horizon forms, the region $r < r_s$ is completely disconnected from the exterior. No information, whether null or timelike, can traverse outward across the horizon. This is a topological property of spacetime itself, not a coordinate artifact.  
* **Interior perspective (proper time):** For matter inside, proper time continues normally until $r=0$. From this perspective, the collapse finishes in finite eigen-time; there is no freezing effect. However, this frame cannot be translated into the exterior description because the horizon is a one-way causal boundary.  
* **No global time parameter:** In the external frame, Schwarzschild time $t$ diverges before anything reaches the horizon, while in the internal frame proper time $\tau$ remains finite. These two times are not transformable into one another by any regular Lorentz mapping; their domains are split by the horizon.

Thus GR already assigns the interior its own causally detached time frame. The reading adopted here diverges only at the extrapolation step: GR continues the classical equations all the way to a singularity, whereas restricting relevance to causally accessible physics leaves the interior endpoint open — it may stabilize or persist under its own differentiations, with causal separation preserved. The external description remains compatible in either case, but the physical end state is not locked to the GR singularity. The mathematical continuation into the interior is a valid formal move within GR; its physical weight becomes questionable once one restricts relevance to causally accessible physics (Interpretation, not a new derivation).

**Three Reality Frames – Physical vs. Abstract**

From this analysis it follows that there are three distinguishable reality frames, though only two of them carry physical relevance:

* **External frame (observable)** – Measurable through astrophysical observation, gravitational effects, and causal accessibility. This is the frame in which collapse remains eternally frozen.  
* **Intrinsic particle frame (point‐relative eigen‐time)** – Physically relevant because every falling particle can in principle measure its own slowing processes; from this perspective it never reaches $r=0$.  
* **GR container frame (abstract interior)** – A purely mathematical continuation inside the horizon, predicting finite proper time to a singularity. It is causally inaccessible, unfalsifiable, and therefore of questionable physical relevance.

**Why Only Two Frames are Physically Real**

The distinction between the three frames exposes the strengths and weaknesses of each description. The external frame is robust because it corresponds to actual measurable observables – gravitational redshift, lensing, orbital dynamics, and signals recorded by telescopes. The intrinsic particle frame is equally robust because it describes a direct physical experience that a local observer would have. The GR container frame, however, exists purely as a formal continuation, not grounded in direct observables.

* **Empirical Accessibility:** The external frame is empirically accessible to astronomers and physicists. The particle-intrinsic frame would be accessible to any infaller, at least in principle, and therefore counts as real. The container frame is not empirically accessible in any way.  
* **Causal Structure:** The external and particle-intrinsic frames both honor causal connectivity: one across astronomical distances, the other locally along the particle’s trajectory. The container frame violates this criterion by describing a causally isolated domain with no outward connection.  
* **Predictive Power:** The external frame predicts phenomena we observe (gravitational redshift, time dilation, horizon behavior). The particle frame predicts local coherence of physics even in extreme fields. The container frame has no predictive power for accessible phenomena.  
* **Theoretical Fragility:** The external and particle frames remain consistent even if GR is modified (quantum corrections, alternative gravity theories). The container frame collapses immediately under modifications that remove singularities, highlighting its lack of robustness.  
* **Philosophical Status:** The external and particle frames describe **worlds we can, in principle, live in.** The container describes a purely abstract extension, a continuation of equations beyond where they are applicable. Its ontological weight is therefore minimal.

Thus, two of the frames carry direct physical content: the external, accessible frame and the particle’s intrinsic, measurable frame. The GR container frame remains a formal mathematical construction, useful for calculation but not part of the causally accessible universe. Read through the scale-differentiation lens, the distinction is natural: differentiation is tied to the reality of frames, and once causal access is cut, a frame drops out of the physically accessible set (Interpretation). The collapse, on this reading, is not toward an absolute singularity but toward an eternal stratification of reality frames, of which only two are directly accessible.

**Loop Quantum Gravity and Bounce Models**

Among established alternative approaches, Loop Quantum Gravity (LQG) bounce models provide a compelling framework that connects directly with the layered, frozen collapse interpretation. In these models, the classical singularity is avoided by introducing a discrete structure of spacetime at the Planck scale.

**Core principles:**

* **Discrete spacetime:** Instead of a smooth continuum, geometry is built from spin networks.  
* **Planck density limit:** When density reaches the Planck scale, quantum effects produce a repulsive component that halts collapse.  
* **Bounce instead of singularity:** The matter that would classically collapse into a singularity instead undergoes a rapid bounce and re-expands, potentially creating a white hole phase.

**Translation to the three frames:**

* In the **external frame**, the black hole still appears eternally frozen at the horizon due to infinite redshift. Any bounce or re-expansion inside is hidden by time dilation and causal separation.  
* In the **particle-intrinsic frame**, the collapsing matter experiences finite proper time to reach Planck density, then undergoes a rapid transition to expansion. For the infaller, this appears nearly instantaneous, like an implosive collapse immediately rebounding.  
* The **GR container frame** is effectively replaced: instead of finite time to a singularity, there is finite time to a quantum bounce. This corrects the breakdown of GR by supplying a physically meaningful process at the point where GR fails.

**Phenomenological consequences:**

* From the outside, black holes behave as in GR for extremely long durations; only at vast timescales might information re-emerge, consistent with a white hole phase.  
* Internally, no singularity forms. The collapse continues into finer scales until the quantum geometry intervenes — a picture compatible with the scale-differentiation reading, where the approach toward zero scale is replaced by continued differentiation at progressively finer levels (Analogy).  
* Information loss is avoided in principle, since data may be released during the white hole outburst, though at timescales that for us are essentially eternal.

**Connection to Scale Differentiation**

* The scale-differentiation reading sets aside absolute singularities in favor of continuing differentiation of scales. LQG bounce models fit naturally with this picture, replacing singular collapse with a transition to new scales of structure (Analogy).  
* The asymptotic freeze of the external frame remains, while internally the dynamics continue in altered form.  
* The LQG bounce can accordingly be read as a quantum-gravitational realization of the same pattern: the approach to zero is never reached, because a new differentiating structure arises at smaller scales.

Within established physics, at least one well-developed alternative to the GR singularity — the LQG bounce — is consistent with this reading: black holes freeze from the outside, while internally avoiding singular collapse through continued structural differentiation. The two pictures are compatible rather than competing.

**Implications for Cosmology and the Big Bang**

This conclusion ties back to the question of cosmic origins. If black holes appear frozen from the outside, with their inner dynamics detached from our causal frame, the assumption of a single, universal beginning such as the classical Big Bang becomes one option among several rather than a forced conclusion (Interpretation). Taking scale differentiation as the common thread across scales, our scale need not be privileged with an absolute beginning when other scales with their own causal disjunctions are possible.

**Key implications:**

* **No preferred scale:** Just as black hole interiors proceed in their own causal frame, disconnected from ours, so too may other scales of reality evolve independently. There is no necessity for our cosmological scale to possess a unique starting point.  
* **Scale-start instead of origin:** What we observe as the Big Bang may be only the onset of differentiation in our frame, not a universal birth of all existence.  
* **CMB reinterpretation:** In LQG/LQH bounce cosmologies, a hot, dense plasma naturally arises after a rebound. Such conditions produce a cosmic microwave background indistinguishable in broad features from our observed CMB. Thus the CMB does not prove a singular Big Bang, only that our scale began in a high-density, high-temperature state.  
* **Frozen parallels:** If black holes freeze from the outside, then other scales can appear to us as eternally suspended. This symmetry argues against privileging our universe with a unique temporal origin.

In sum, under the scale-differentiation reading the Big Bang is better described as a localized differentiation process in our scale — one among the configurations the picture allows — rather than as an absolute universal event (Interpretation). Black hole freezing illustrates that causally isolated regimes are possible, which weakens any claim of exclusivity for our cosmological beginning. The CMB remains consistent with this reading, representing the thermodynamic signature of our scale’s initiation rather than evidence of an ultimate origin.

Taken together, the frozen collapse of black holes, the causal separation of internal and external frames, the resolution of asymmetry through gravitational radiation, the frame classification introduced above, and the LQG bounce treatment all point in a compatible direction. On this reading, singularities are not required as physical endpoints; what is tracked are stratified frames of differentiation, each valid within its causal domain (Interpretation). From the external perspective, collapse is effectively frozen; from the internal perspective, differentiation continues without reaching a zero scale. The same pattern appears elsewhere in the scale-differentiation reading: a description built around endless differentiation across scales rather than a single origin or end.

Applied to cosmology, the same reading softens the claim of a privileged Big Bang origin to one option among several (Interpretation). Just as black hole interiors carry causally isolated dynamics that are not observable from outside, other scales may likewise exist beyond our causal access. The cosmic microwave background is, on this reading, the thermodynamic signature of our scale’s initiation rather than a universal beginning of all reality. The resulting picture is one of ongoing differentiation across scales without absolute singularities or a privileged origin, with black holes acting as accessible local examples of the same pattern.
